A controller online is neither an opponent nor an examiner — they are the person responsible for the safety of the shared flow. Let us look at who is who and how to accept instructions properly.

## Who is who on frequency

- **DEL (delivery)** — issues the IFR clearance and the agreed route.
- **GND (ground)** — handles taxiing on the aerodrome and releases you for takeoff.
- **TWR (tower)** — accepts takeoffs and landings and owns the runway.
- **APP (approach)** — runs the sequence, gives vectors and sets descent levels.
- **CTR (center)** — works the en-route area: track, flight levels and handoffs between sectors.

Frequencies change in order: delivery → ground → tower → departure/approach → center → arrival → ground. A new controller callsign means a new phase of the flight.

## How to read back an instruction

The formula is **read back, then act**:

1. Listen to the instruction to the end without interrupting.
2. Read it back in the same order: "Runway 27 via Charlie, climb and maintain 5000, SkyNational 1234".
3. Only after the controller confirms do you act.

The answer is short, and use future tense where that is the convention: "wilco" for "understood", a full readback for numbers and levels. If your readback is wrong, you will be corrected — that is a normal part of the process.

## Typical instructions

- **Startup / clearance** — "Cleared to destination via flight plan, flight level 120": accept the route and the level.
- **Taxi** — "Taxi to runway 27 via Alpha, hold short of runway 09": follow the layout and stop before the runway you must not cross.
- **Lineup and wait** — occupy the runway and hold; taking off without a separate clearance is not allowed.
- **Climb / descend and maintain** — climb or descend strictly to the stated altitude.
- **Contact next frequency** — switch to the named frequency and start with your own callsign.
- **Expect vectors / visual** — expect vectors for the approach or a visual circuit to the runway.

## If you missed the frequency or were busy

Do not go silent: wait a couple of seconds and transmit "standing by", or simply repeat your callsign. Asking for a "say again" is not a violation; staying quiet is. If you are unsure about an instruction, carry out the safe part and clarify, changing nothing abruptly.

## Requesting a deviation from the route

Any deviation — weather avoidance, moving off the track — is requested in advance: "Request deviation 10 degrees right due to weather". The controller will either approve it or offer an alternative. Changing the track on your own inside their area of responsibility is not acceptable.

## When there is no controller

No controller means no restrictions, but it still means responsibility. Request **flight following** if the service is available, and where no service exists at all, use **UNICOM**: listen to the frequency, broadcast your intentions on the standard station and give way. The absence of a controller does not cancel the rule about not cutting across someone else's approach, nor does it cancel common sense.
