VATSIM is the network most newcomers join first. Let us look at how it differs from offline flying and walk through everything from registration to your first connection.

## What VATSIM is

VATSIM is an international virtual aviation network where pilots and air traffic controllers work in a shared radio environment under real-world rules. The key difference from offline flying is simple: you are not alone in an empty world. There is live traffic around you, and controllers are on duty above major airports, accepting flight plans and issuing clearances.

Your job as a newcomer is not to be fast — it is to be accurate: fit into the flow, keep the contact going and follow instructions. That is why it pays to learn the basics of radio phraseology and planning before you go online.

## Registration and choosing a callsign

1. Open the VATSIM website and register an account: country, email address, password.
2. Fill in your profile and state your experience level if the network asks for it.
3. Get a callsign. By default you are issued a four-letter callsign with a number — for example, AB1234. You can fly with it right away.
4. A **registered aircraft callsign** (aircraft registration, company prefix or a personal registrant) is applied for separately in your account settings. It is not required to start.

A callsign must be free: the same one cannot be used in two connections at the same time.

## Installing the network client

A network client (vPilot, xPilot and similar) is a small program that links your simulator to the network, draws other traffic and transmits your own data.

- Download the client from the official network resource and install it.
- Start it, log in with your account and allow the program to access your simulator.
- Check the status: the client must confirm that the simulator has been detected.

## Linking your simulator account

So that the network knows which simulator and aircraft you are flying, link your simulator account (Microsoft Flight Simulator, X-Plane, Prepar3D) in the client settings or in your account area. Some simulators need an extra bridge — SimConnect or FSUIPC. The essential software article covers this in detail.

## Your first connection

1. Load the aircraft on a stand at the departure airport **before** you connect to the network.
2. Start the client, enter your callsign, aircraft type and departure airport.
3. Connect and listen to the frequency for a few minutes before you transmit.
4. If voice still feels intimidating, start in text mode — it is fully accepted on the network.

## Pre-flight checklist before going online

- The flight plan is ready: route, flight level, fuel, alternate airport.
- Weather and NOTAMs have been reviewed for departure, en route and arrival.
- Callsign, aircraft type and radio settings are checked in the client.
- Frequencies are mapped out: where the controller is and when to expect a handoff.
- You have time: do not connect if you are in a hurry.
